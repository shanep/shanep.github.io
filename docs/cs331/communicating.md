# 0.01 Communicating with Each Other

## Communicating Online

Even though many of us are very accustomed to communicating via email, text, and discussion
forum, there is still a lot that can get lost in translation when communicating online. The
standards of professionalism and kindness that many have for in-person conversations do not always
show up in digital ones. Because nearly all communication in this class happens through email and
Canvas discussions, it is crucial that we stay aware of the challenges of talking online and make
every effort to remain professional, courteous, and clear.

We will follow these guidelines. Please contact me if you have questions, or believe anyone is not
acting in accordance with them.

- Be positive and respectful of others' thoughts, ideas, and contributions.
- Avoid texting or slang language.
- Use effective communication by clearly saying what you mean.
- Practice good manners and thank others for their contributions.
- Ask for clarification if you are unclear about what you read.

For more, see Boise State's
[When Communicating in Your Online Class](https://www.boisestate.edu/online/admitted/communicating-online/)
page.

## Discussion Guidelines

A security course adds three rules of its own, which apply to every discussion in it:

- **Do not name real individuals** as the cause of an incident, including yourself. Describe roles
  and systems.
- **Do not post anything confidential** from a current or former employer. Change identifying
  details.
- **Do not post working attacks** against any system, including your own.

## When to Message Me

General questions about the course, an assignment, or a due date belong in the **Course
Questions** discussion, where the answer helps everyone. Anything about your own grade, or any
other personal matter, goes to me directly by email or Canvas message, as the
[Communication Policy](index.md#communication-policy) describes.
