# D6: Final Reflection

**Finals week · 15 points · about 45 minutes · Canvas discussion board**

## What this is

A short, honest look back at the seven objectives this course set out to teach. It is graded on
thoughtfulness and specificity, not on how well you say you did. Saying "I still do not really
understand X" is worth full credit if you can say *what* about X and *where* you lost the thread.
That is genuinely useful to me for the next offering.

There is no right answer here and no reason to perform confidence.

## Objectives assessed

This is a **self-assessment against all seven terminal objectives**. It does not score any single
objective: it asks you to judge your own position against
[all of them](../objectives.md). It counts toward the discussions and reflections category.

## Before you start

Open [objectives.md](../objectives.md) and read the seven terminal objectives and the 34 supporting
objectives under them. Then skim your own graded work from the semester, the labs are the fastest
way to remember what you actually did.

## Post

**Due: the last day of finals week. About 400-500 words. No replies required.**

Four parts.

### 1. Strongest and weakest

Name **the terminal objective you are most confident about** and **the one you are least confident
about**. For each, name a **specific supporting objective** (by number) and say what you can or
cannot do.

Not: "I understand cryptography." Instead: "I can do 3.4 (I can explain what a failed signature
verification does and does not prove), but 3.5 is shaky; I can read a certificate chain but I could
not explain to somebody what actually goes wrong if a CA is compromised."

### 2. The thing that changed how you see something

Name **one** idea from this course that changed how you look at something outside the course, a
product you use, a system at work, a news story, an argument you had. Say what you thought before
and what you think now.

If nothing did, say that, and say what you think the reason is. That is a real answer and I would
rather have it than an invented one.

### 3. The hardest part, and why

Name the week, the lab, or the concept that was hardest, and diagnose it. Was it the reading, the
tool, the maths, the writing, the time, or the fact that it built on something you had not
understood two weeks earlier? Be specific enough that I could act on it.

### 4. What you would tell the next section

One paragraph addressed to a student starting this course in a future semester. What would you tell
them to do differently in week 1? What did you spend time on that did not pay off? What did you skip
that you should not have?
